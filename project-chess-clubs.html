Chess club and tournament manager

A Django application for running chess clubs and the tournaments they hold. A five-person group project during my BSc — what follows is the part I worked on.

  • Python
  • Django
  • Group project
  • 2021

The system

Clubs have members in different roles, and people apply to join rather than joining outright. Members enter the tournaments their club runs, and the application organises the tournament itself — who plays whom, in which stage, and what happens as results come in.

What I worked on

Work was picked up card by card rather than split into strict ownership, so this is the set of tickets I took rather than a clean area of the codebase.

  • Clubs and membership

    • The applicant model, so joining a club is a request someone approves rather than an instant action.
    • Member and club listings, and the information pages behind them.
    • An indicator on the member list showing each person’s role within the club.
    • A navbar that lets you pick a club and takes you to it, giving the app a sense of which club you are currently in.

  • Tournaments

    • Tournament list and information views.
    • Entry closing at the deadline, so nobody can join a tournament that has already been drawn.
    • The organiser arranging and publishing the schedule of matches in the window after entries close but before play starts.

  • The tournament engine

    • The match and match group models.
    • Later stages for larger fields — 17 to 32 entrants, and 33 to 96 — which cannot run as a single bracket.
    • Group stages scored on wins, losses and draws, with the top two from each group going through.
    • Pairing that keeps the same two players apart until the structure makes meeting again unavoidable.

  • Tests

    • The user class models, the member list, the applicant and club lists.
    • The tournament list and the create-tournament view.
    • Extensions to the applicant list.

The part worth talking about

The scheduling. A 96-entrant tournament cannot be one bracket, so the field has to be cut down in stages, and each stage needs a format that suits however many players are left in it.

Sitting on top of that is the constraint that two players should not meet again until the structure forces it — easy to state, and considerably more fiddly to actually satisfy while the groups for the next stage are still being formed.
