Game tracking and reviews

Letterboxd, but for video games: log what you have played and what you want to play, rate and review it, and follow other people. A six-person group project during my BSc — what follows is the part I worked on.

  • Python
  • Django
  • Group project

The site

The brief was open — the team chose its own deliverables — and what we settled on was a games equivalent of Letterboxd. People record the games they have played or want to play, score and review them, gather them into collections, and follow each other to see what everyone else is playing.

What I worked on

Work was picked up card by card rather than split into strict ownership, so this is the set of tickets I took.

  • People and the follow graph

    • The user model.
    • Following other users, and the followers and following lists that come with it.
    • The page listing all users, and a seeder to populate them for development.

  • Games, ratings and collections

    • The individual game page, and the page listing every game.
    • The form for rating and reviewing a game, capped at one review per person per game.
    • The view showing every review a game has received.
    • Creating collections of games.

  • Finding things, and shipping it

    • A search bar covering both games and users.
    • Getting the project uploaded, and writing the setup instructions.
    • The section of the report covering what the team had chosen to deliver.

Two bugs worth keeping

Both were the same mistake in different clothes: the data model kept two facts apart, and the code quietly treated them as one.

Zero-star reviews were disappearing. A review scored zero showed up nowhere on the game’s page. Zero is a perfectly valid score, but somewhere between the query and the template it was being read as no score at all — so the harshest reviews were the ones nobody could see.

Played is not reviewed. The method fetching a game’s reviews was returning every play record, including games someone had logged as played but never written anything about. Review lists were padded with entries that had nothing to display, because a query had merged two states the model deliberately kept separate.
